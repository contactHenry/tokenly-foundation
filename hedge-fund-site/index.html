For allocators

Questions we hear in the first meeting.

Full terms are set out in each fund's offering documents, which we share after an introductory call.

Who can invest with Vesperline?

Our funds are open only to institutional investors and to individuals who meet the professional, accredited or qualified-purchaser tests in their jurisdiction. We do not accept retail investors and we do not market to the general public.

What is the minimum investment?

The minimum initial subscription is $5 million for the flagship fund and $10 million for single-strategy funds. Separately managed accounts start at $250 million.

How often can investors redeem?

The flagship fund offers quarterly liquidity with 60 days' notice and a 25% investor-level gate. Credit Opportunities has annual liquidity to match the assets it holds.

How are fees structured?

A management fee that steps down with commitment size, and an incentive fee charged only on gains above a high-water mark. Founders' and long-lock share classes carry lower fees.

Where are the funds domiciled?

Master-feeder structures with Cayman Islands and Delaware feeders, and an Irish ICAV for European investors. Funds are administered and audited by independent third parties.

The firm

Built to be early, and to stay solvent while it waits.

Vesperline was founded in 2009 by three portfolio managers who had watched good trades fail because the capital behind them could not wait. We designed the firm around one idea: match the horizon of the capital to the horizon of the opportunity.

Approach

We decide how much we can lose before we decide what to buy.

Our process is written down, the same in every office, and audited each year. These are its three stages and the limits that sit around it.

STAGE 1

Thesis

A portfolio manager writes a memo: why the price is wrong, what would prove it right, what would prove it wrong, and when we will look again.

STAGE 2

Sizing

Risk sets a loss budget from the memo's downside case, stress tests and market liquidity. Size follows from the budget, never from conviction alone.

STAGE 3

Review

Every position is reviewed on its stated date or when it uses half its loss budget, whichever comes first. A failed thesis is closed, not averaged down.

The cost of being early

Eleanor Ashdown, CIO14 October 20269 min read

Being early and being wrong look the same on a monthly statement. The difference only shows up later, and only if you are still holding the position when it does. This quarter tested that idea in two of our books, and we want to explain what we held, what we sold and why.

The quarter in brief

The Multi-Strategy fund finished the quarter slightly positive after a drawdown in August. Global Macro and Credit Opportunities contributed; Systematic Equity was flat after a difficult period for short-horizon signals in July.

“A loss budget is a promise to ourselves, made before the market has a chance to argue.”

Where we were early

We began buying long-dated Japanese government bond volatility in May. For ten weeks the position lost money as the market priced a slow, orderly exit from yield-curve control. In August it paid for itself in four trading days. We sized it so that the ten weeks would cost no more than a third of its loss budget, and that is what let us stay in.

Our European mid-market loan book is in the same position today. We think refinancing pressure in 2027 and 2028 is under-priced, and we are building positions slowly. We expect them to cost us before they help us.
